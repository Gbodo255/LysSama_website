### The invisible founder problem

We work with founders who spend six figures on a rebrand and then refuse to appear anywhere on the site. Their story is the reason the business exists. Their point of view is what the customer is actually buying. And yet the About page shows a stock photo of a laptop on a wooden desk and a paragraph of corporate we-language.

The pattern is so common we now expect it. Founders are trained by decades of corporate culture to remove themselves from the brand as it grows. That instinct is the opposite of what a small brand actually needs.

### What the buyer is actually buying

For small brands, the buyer is not just buying the product. They are buying the reason the product exists, and reason has a face. The founder's story is not filler on the About page. It is the credibility engine of the whole brand.

A candle brand from Savannah is more compelling because a real person in Savannah is pouring the candles. A leather workshop in Milan is more compelling because a family in Milan is cutting the leather. Anonymise either brand and the value proposition weakens. Put the founder back in and it strengthens immediately.

### How we surface the founder

On every Mira project, we push for a founder moment near the top of the homepage. A photo, a short story, a signed statement. Not a full About page relocated to the top. Just enough presence to establish that a real person is behind the brand. It changes the emotional register of everything below it.

Some founders resist this at first. They worry it looks unprofessional. It does not. What looks unprofessional is a small brand pretending to be a big one.

### Why this is the unfair advantage

The competition is well-funded, well-designed, and personality-free. Big brands cannot use their founders because their founders are usually a board of directors. Small brands can, and most refuse to. That refusal is the single biggest gift small brands hand to their competitors every day.

Show up. Say what you believe. Sign your name. The founder is your unfair advantage. Use them.
