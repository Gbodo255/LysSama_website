### Two different audiences

Awards juries and paying customers are not the same audience. Juries are made up of other designers, judging the work against other work in the same category, at the same show, in the same year. They reward novelty, craft, and technical ambition. That is not a bad thing. It just is not the same test the market is running.

The buyer at the other end is not asking whether a site looks like it deserves a Site of the Day badge. They are asking whether the brand feels credible enough to trust with a purchase decision. Different question, different answer.

### What awards actually reward

Sites that win Awwwards or FWA usually share a few traits: an ambitious hero interaction, a strong visual signature, a moment of technical craft that stops the scroll. Those traits are wonderful. They are also, in most cases, expensive to build, hard to maintain, and mostly invisible to the person who actually needs to convert.

We know because we have shipped both. The projects with the biggest business outcomes rarely had the flashiest craft moments. The projects with the flashiest craft moments rarely had the biggest business outcomes. This is not an accident.

### Where they overlap

Occasionally, the same project wins both games. A hero that is both cinematic and commercially precise. A brand system that is both jury-friendly and clear enough to sell. Those are the projects everyone celebrates. They are rare, and they usually happen when the studio and client are aligned on what matters before the work starts.

### What we tell clients

We tell clients honestly: awards are for us, not for you. If the work is jury-friendly, we will submit. If it is not, we will not, and we will not compromise the effectiveness of the work to make it juryable. The client is paying for outcomes. The award is a bonus if the work happens to earn one. It is not the goal.
