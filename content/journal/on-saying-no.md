### The three we turned down this year

A Series B SaaS that wanted us to speed up their sales cycle through a rebrand. A fashion label pivoting mid-launch who could not describe the pivot in a sentence. A founder with a compelling story who could not articulate what the business actually sold. Three real inbound conversations. Three no's.

Each of those was a real revenue opportunity. Each of them was also a project that would have quietly compromised the studio, either by pulling attention from a better-fit engagement or by demanding a scope we did not think we could deliver honestly.

### What every no protects

The maths of a small studio is unforgiving. If we take on the wrong project, it does not just affect that project. It steals capacity from the next two projects, because the founder is now debugging a bad brief instead of pushing the right one forward. Every no protects the yes.

Studios that say yes to everything eventually stop being good at anything. We watched a couple of studios we admire drift this way in the last three years. We do not want to be next.

### The habit is harder than it sounds

Saying no is not a skill studios teach. Every referral feels like an obligation to the person referring. Every quick project feels like it might turn into something bigger later. Every founder pitching feels sincere in the moment. All of that is real, and none of it changes whether the project is a fit.

Getting comfortable with no took us years. We had to learn to say it kindly, quickly, and with a clear reason. We had to learn to not sugarcoat it into a maybe.

### Why the work is better for it

The projects on our site now are the ones we said yes to for the right reasons. Every one of them had a clear brief, a client who was ready, and a scope we knew we could ship well. That is not accident. That is the compounding effect of a hundred quiet nos.
